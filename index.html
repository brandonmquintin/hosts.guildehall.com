00. For venue hosts

GuildeHall lists event venues owned or run by nonprofits and community groups in central Virginia. Member venues take bookings through GuildeHall, which handles each one from the renter's request to the return of the security deposit, while the venue keeps every decision about its building.

This site explains how membership works, what it costs, and what GuildeHall needs to set it up. It is written for the board members, rental committees, and staff who would manage the rentals.

01. What GuildeHall is

GuildeHall is a directory of Virginia's clubs, societies, and civic groups, their public events, and the places they meet. Its Venue Directory, at guildehall.com/venues, lists about thirty event spaces in central Virginia, among them theaters, museums, historic churches, lodge halls, and Ruritan parks. Every one is owned or run by a nonprofit or community group, and the rent goes to that organization.

Renters use it to find a place for a wedding, a reunion, a fundraiser, a meeting, or a company retreat. They browse a map or filter by county, and each listing shows photographs, the spaces for rent, capacity, and the organization behind the venue. GuildeHall doesn't list venues owned by commercial landlords or event companies.

02. Two kinds of listing

A venue appears in the directory in one of two ways.

A member venue takes bookings through GuildeHall. Its listing shows your prices, a calendar of open dates, your rules, and a Request booking button, and GuildeHall manages each booking from the request to the return of the deposit. A member venue pays 5% of each rental, and the renter pays a separate 5% service fee.

A free listing shows your venue to renters and passes their questions on to you. You set prices, take bookings, and collect payment as you do now, and GuildeHall charges nothing.

A venue can move from one kind of listing to the other at any time.

03. Member venues

A member venue hands GuildeHall the paperwork of renting. GuildeHall answers renters' questions, collects the details of each event, has the renter sign your rental agreement, takes payment and the deposit, arranges insurance for renters who need it, and returns the deposit afterward. You approve every booking.

The listing shows your prices and an estimate for the dates a renter picks, the deposit, the spaces and capacity, your house rules, and a preview of your rental agreement. Renters see your terms before they can request a date.

You won't have GuildeHall software to learn or log in to. The one account you need is with Stripe, the payment company, which pays your share of each rental into your organization's bank account.

04. What it costs

A member venue pays no setup fee and no subscription. GuildeHall is paid only from bookings. The renter pays a 5% service fee on top of your rental price, and GuildeHall keeps 5% of the rental price from your payout. The deposit carries no fee. Card and bank processing fees come out of GuildeHall's share, so neither you nor the renter pays them separately.

On a $1,000 rental with a $200 deposit, the renter pays $1,250: the rental, a $50 service fee, and the deposit. Your organization receives $950 when the renter pays. GuildeHall holds the deposit until after the event, then returns it to the renter, less anything you keep for damage, which comes to you.

You set your own cancellation policy. When a booking is cancelled and the renter is refunded, GuildeHall refunds its fees as well.

A free listing costs nothing.

05. How a booking works
  1. The renter picks a space and a date at least two weeks away, then sends a request describing the event: what it is, how many guests, who is holding it, whether they already carry liability insurance, and the answers to any questions you've added.
  2. GuildeHall sends the request to your booking contact and follows up by phone or email, whichever that person prefers. You approve or decline it within a week, which is what renters are told to expect, and confirm the final price, since the figure on the listing is an estimate. If you want to show the renter the space first, the tour happens now.
  3. The renter reads and signs your rental agreement online. GuildeHall records who signed it and when, and lets you know.
  4. The renter pays the rental, the service fee, and the deposit in one payment, by card or bank transfer. Your share reaches your organization's bank account through Stripe, with no checks to deposit.
  5. If you require insurance, a renter without a policy buys one through a form GuildeHall sends with the event's details already filled in. A renter with a policy gives you the certificate.
  6. After the event, you say how much of the deposit to keep, if any. The rest is refunded to the renter, and you receive the part you keep.

06. Insurance

You decide whether renters must carry event liability insurance, and how much coverage they need, commonly $1,000,000. Venue owners GuildeHall has spoken with often name insurance as the hardest part of renting to the public. Many renters don't know they need it, and those who do often don't know where to buy it.

Every booking request asks the renter whether they already have a policy. A renter who does gives the certificate to you. A renter who doesn't receives a link, once they've paid, to buy a policy from Event Helper, an event insurance company, with the renter's name, the venue, and the dates already entered. That policy is recorded on the booking, and the certificate goes to your booking contact.

If you require insurance and the renter never provides it, tell GuildeHall. The booking is cancelled, and the renter is refunded under your cancellation policy.

07. What you decide

At a member venue you decide:

  • Which dates are open, and which spaces you rent
  • What you charge, and the amount of the deposit
  • Which kinds of events you accept
  • The rules renters must follow in your building
  • Your cancellation policy
  • Whether renters need insurance, and how much

You can decline any request.

Dates booked through GuildeHall close on your listing's calendar automatically. If you keep a calendar for the venue in Google Calendar or Apple Calendar, share its private link with GuildeHall, which imports the dates you block there. GuildeHall doesn't write to your calendar, so add each booking it emails you to your own calendar to keep the date from being booked twice.

08. What to send

A member venue starts with what any listing needs: photographs you have the right to share, the address, capacity, the spaces you rent, a description of the venue, and a few sentences about your organization. If your venue already has a free listing, most of that is done. GuildeHall also needs:

  • Your prices and how they work (by the day, by the weekend, or by the number of guests), any booking fee, and the deposit
  • Your rental agreement, including your cancellation policy
  • Whether you require insurance, and the coverage
  • Your policies on kitchen use, food and drink, alcohol, restrooms, furniture, parking, accessibility, and anything else renters must observe
  • A booking contact, who will approve requests, and whether that person prefers a call or an email
  • The dates already taken, or the link to your venue calendar
  • A Stripe account for your organization

Start with the Stripe account, because it takes longest. You'll receive a link to set it up. Stripe asks for the organization's legal name and EIN, a bank account for payouts, and personal details for one officer who will be responsible for the account. If Stripe asks for documents to confirm them, allow a few more days.

When everything is in, GuildeHall builds your listing and agreement, shows you the page, runs a test booking from request to payment, and then opens your calendar to renters.

09. Free listings

A free listing shows your photographs, address, capacity, rental spaces, and a description of the venue, with a short paragraph about your organization, a map, and a link to the rental page on your own website.

Renters reach you through the Inquire button. They give a name, an email address, and, if they like, a phone number and a message. The inquiry is emailed to the address you choose, with a copy to GuildeHall, and your reply goes straight to the renter.

Your venue may already be listed. GuildeHall has built some listings from public information and photographs, and each one says so on the page. Until you claim the listing, inquiries come to GuildeHall, which forwards them to you. To claim it, add your own photographs and description, or have it taken down, write to admin@guildehall.com. Takedowns happen the same day.

10. Who can list

A venue can join GuildeHall if:

  • It is available to rent to the public, or its owner is willing to make it so
  • It is owned or run by a nonprofit or other noncommercial organization
  • It is in central Virginia, roughly the area between Fredericksburg, Charlottesville, Williamsburg, and Petersburg

GuildeHall confirms each organization's nonprofit status before listing it.

11. Getting started

To talk about becoming a member venue, list your venue, or claim a listing that's already up, write to contact@guildehall.com.

Brandon Quintin, GuildeHall's founder, reads every message and sets up each member venue himself. He is glad to visit the venue and go over any of this with your board.
